# Cinematic game and anime PV direction

Start with one audience reaction and one specific promise. Plan the emotional progression, contrast, music phrasing and evidence before adding effects. Define the look using a few strong references and original subject-specific decisions; avoid deriving the whole film from a template's palette or typeface.

## Choose the cut

| Deliverable | Useful shape | Review focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game announcement | Evocative opening → world/stakes → central fantasy → title/payoff | An unmistakable world identity; honest development/cinematic labels |
| Gameplay or launch trailer | Immediate player verb → readable loop/systems → escalating challenge → reward → CTA | Genuine capture, purposeful pacing, legible action and a current build |
| Anime character PV | Identity detail → silhouette/personality → signature action → emotional turn → iconic key visual/title | Character consistency, expressive acting, costume/weapon continuity and deliberate voice direction |
| Game website hero | One dominant composition with spatial depth and a seamless visual breath | Quiet copy-safe zone, loop boundary, first-frame poster and compression quality |

These are patterns, not mandated shot counts or durations. Match the brief and music rather than filling a fixed template.

## Direction bible and shot acceptance

Record subject invariants, shape language, palette/lighting relationships, type hierarchy, camera/lens behavior, movement vocabulary, texture/grain policy, music/voice intent and examples of what fails. For each shot, specify the beat, source asset, composition, focal attention, camera path, depth layers, action timing, transition, exact text/audio and visible acceptance criteria. Link dependent shots to an approved parent artwork or model.

Use contrast across sequences: wide against close, stillness against action, atmosphere against clean graphic space, dark against a controlled highlight, and a short silence against musical impact. Keep effects and chromatic aberration purposeful. Avoid a continuous wall of bloom, particles, shakes and text flying at the camera; that can obscure both the character and the game's main idea.

Treat transitions as movements with a reason: follow an action direction, match a shape, change focus, bridge through light/color, or hard-cut on a beat. Declare hard cuts in the renderer. Give important copy stable readable dwell time; do not use motion blur to mask poor layout. Keep a clear visual hierarchy at phone size and at 100% crop.

## Character fidelity

Keep approved full-body, face, expression, costume and accessory references available for all shots. Check face and costume through motion and transitions, not only in the first frame. Track hair groups, clothing overlap, hand/weapon contact, pose silhouette and intentional state changes. When using layered key art, choose purposeful parallax distances and avoid stretching the face or turning flat artwork so far that it exposes the illusion. For acting, use rigged/engine animation or reviewed video generation rather than promising HTML alone will produce it.

If a generated shot changes a defining detail, repair or replace the affected shot. Do not change the character bible to fit an accidental output. Keep approved narration/voice wording and persona consistent; align captions with speech and meaningful sound.

## Sound and finishing

Choose music whose phrasing supports the beat map. Separate music, VO and SFX stems; duck competing elements and leave room for the title/payoff. Inspect the exported soundtrack for clipped peaks, abrupt cuts, unintelligible dialogue and unwanted silence. Use destination-specific loudness guidance when specified; do not impose one universal target on every platform.

Inspect a short full-quality proof before rendering the entire PV. Judge the encoded MP4 for compression damage in fog, dark gradients, line art and particles. Review the actual opening, transition extrema and ending, then watch the complete export with sound. Technical gates catch export failures; they cannot certify emotional impact or visual taste. Record unresolved creative findings explicitly and keep the project in draft until reviewed.
